2The big ideas

A language model is billions of learned numbers. Nobody wrote them by hand, so even its builders cannot simply read off how it works. Interpretability is the science of finding out.

superposition

More ideas than neurons

Models pack far more concepts than they have neurons, as overlapping directions. That is why a single neuron can fire for citations, dialogue, web requests and Korean text. Shown clearly in toy models; well supported, not proven, in large ones.

dictionaries

Unpacking the concepts

Sparse autoencoders rewrite a model's activity as a few active concepts out of a large dictionary. “Just 512 neurons can represent tens of thousands of features.” Later: up to 34 million features in Claude 3 Sonnet, 16 million in GPT-4.

steering

Features cause behaviour

Turn up the Golden Gate Bridge feature and Claude starts to believe it is the bridge. Anthropic ran this as a public demo for 24 hours in May 2024.

circuits

Tracing a thought

Inside Claude 3.5 Haiku you can see “Texas” on the way from “Dallas” to “Austin”, rhymes planned before a line is written, and two parallel paths for mental arithmetic.

languages

One mind, many languages

Much of the circuitry for a concept is shared across languages. A larger model shared more than twice the proportion of features as a smaller one, good news for languages with less data.

safety

Useful for safety

In a blind audit, 3 of 4 teams found a model's hidden objective; the team with interpretability tools was fastest (1.5 h), though searching training data also worked. Interpretability is now part of some pre-release safety testing.

4Honest about the limits

Think of it as the first maps of a new continent: real, useful, and still mostly blank.

coverage

A small fraction

Even the best studies capture only a small part of what a model computes, on short prompts, after hours of human work.

dark matter

Unexplained activity

Dictionaries leave part of the model's activity unexplained, and bigger dictionaries tend to miss the same things.

illusions

Right answer, wrong reason

An intervention can appear to work while acting through a hidden parallel path.

simple wins

Probes can beat dictionaries

Google DeepMind reported that a simple linear probe beat sparse autoencoders on detecting harmful intent, and deprioritised that line of work.

The goal many researchers share is an “MRI for AI” that can reliably find problems before deployment. Anthropic has set a goal for 2027: an aspiration, not an achievement.
